# Imperium Founder Investment Canon

Version: 0.1
Status: Draft canon
Source influence: Investing in SUPERIOR Companies by Chris Birrell
Owner: Founder / CTO

## Purpose

This document translates the investment logic of `Investing in SUPERIOR Companies` into the founder-incubator operating doctrine for the ZenCloudAU estate.

The purpose is not to copy the SUPERIOR framework. The purpose is to honour its practitioner logic, extract what applies to our founder-incubator model, and convert it into a Roman/Latin estate canon that can guide productisation, venture selection, repo governance, and spin-out decisions.

The source framework is used as a mentor text. The estate doctrine must become our own.

## Source pattern

The SUPERIOR framework evaluates companies through eight lenses and five aspects.

The eight source lenses are:

| SUPERIOR lens | Meaning |
|---|---|
| S | Solve a difficult problem |
| U | Uncover novel solutions |
| P | Build a profitable business |
| E | Leverage ecosystems |
| R | Overcome regional constraints |
| I | Create impact |
| O | Protect ownership |
| R | Ride regulatory tailwinds |

The five aspects are:

| Aspect | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Headlines | The essence of the company through that lens |
| Founders | Founder fit, domain insight, conviction, judgement |
| Business model | How value converts into durable economics |
| Market | Whether the market can pull the product forward |
| Traction | Whether early evidence supports the thesis |

The strongest source insight is that good evaluation is multi-perspective. One view is not enough. A serious company, product, or founder must be examined through repeated passes from different angles.

## Applicability to ZenCloudAU

The first five SUPER lenses are immediately applicable to the founder incubator estate.

They ask the questions every future product must answer before it becomes a deployed app, venture line, or commercial surface.

| Source lens | Estate translation |
|---|---|
| Solve a difficult problem | Does this product solve a painful, meaningful, hard problem? |
| Uncover novel solutions | Is the mechanism genuinely different or merely another interface? |
| Profitable business | Can this become economically durable, not just interesting? |
| Ecosystems | Does it attach to or create a larger system of value? |
| Regional constraints | Does locality, regulation, language, domain, or market context create advantage? |

The final three lenses are still important, but they mature later in the estate lifecycle.

| Source lens | Estate translation |
|---|---|
| Impact | Does this product improve human capability, clarity, judgement, or outcomes? |
| Ownership | Is there defensible IP, doctrine, data, workflow, brand, or system memory? |
| Regulation | Does law, governance, risk, compliance, or institutional pressure create a tailwind? |

## Roman conversion

The ZenCloudAU estate will not use generic startup language as its primary doctrine. It will use Roman/Latin language where appropriate, with plain English explanation for usability.

The derived canon is called `Imperium`.

Imperium is not used here as domination. It is used as command, authority, jurisdiction, and disciplined capability.

## The Imperium Canon

| Imperium lens | Latin anchor | Source relationship | Estate question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Incendium | Solve a difficult problem | Is there a hair-on-fire problem that compels the product to exist? |
| M | Machina | Uncover novel solutions | What mechanism, engine, or system makes this solution non-obvious? |
| P | Pecunia | Profitable business | How does value convert into durable economics? |
| E | Ecosystema | Leverage ecosystems | What ecosystem does this product attach to, enable, or create? |
| R | Regio | Overcome regional constraints | What local, domain, cultural, or regulatory constraint becomes advantage? |
| I | Impactus | Create impact | What human or institutional capability is improved? |
| U | Unicum | Protect ownership | What is uniquely owned, remembered, encoded, or defensible? |
| M | Mandatum | Ride regulatory tailwinds | What governance, compliance, mandate, or institutional pressure supports adoption? |

## The five aspects become the Five Tables

The SUPERIOR framework uses five aspects: Headlines, Founders, Business Model, Market, and Traction.

In the ZenCloudAU estate, these become the Five Tables.

| Table | Meaning | Estate use |
|---|---|---|
| Titulus | Headline | The plain claim of the product |
| Auctor | Founder | Founder fit, domain truth, conviction, taste |
| Ratio | Business model | Economic logic and pricing path |
| Forum | Market | Audience, buyer, channel, ecosystem demand |
| Signum | Traction | Evidence, signal, validation, usage, proof |

## Imperium grid

A serious product or venture candidate should be assessed through each Imperium lens and each Table.

This creates forty checks, but not every check must be equally deep at every stage.

Early stage products should focus first on:

- Incendium
- Machina
- Pecunia
- Ecosystema
- Regio

Mature product candidates should later add:

- Impactus
- Unicum
- Mandatum

## Incubation alignment

| Estate stage | Imperium emphasis |
|---|---|
| Spark | Incendium and Machina only |
| Concept | Incendium, Machina, Titulus, Auctor |
| Prototype | Machina, Forum, Signum |
| Exploratio | Incendium, Pecunia, Forum, Signum |
| Product | Pecunia, Ecosystema, Unicum |
| Venture | Full Imperium review |

Note: `Exploratio` replaces modern terms such as pilot or proof of concept in the Latin doctrine.

## Productisation test

Before any repo, tool, or idea becomes a productised app, it must pass the following test.

| Question | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Incendium | The problem is painful enough to justify repeated use |
| Machina | The solution has a distinct mechanism or workflow |
| Pecunia | There is a credible payment, usage, subscription, service, or licensing path |
| Ecosystema | The product connects to a broader system of value |
| Regio | The product has a domain, regional, cultural, or institutional advantage |
| Impactus | The product makes users clearer, faster, safer, stronger, or more capable |
| Unicum | There is protectable IP, workflow memory, doctrine, brand, or data |
| Mandatum | There is a regulatory, governance, organisational, or institutional pressure that supports adoption |

## Founder-investor use

The canon supports two roles.

As founder, it asks: should I build this, productise this, deploy this, or stop?

As angel investor, it asks: should I believe this founder, this market, this mechanism, and this economic path?

This is why the canon matters. It becomes both product governance and investment judgement.

## Relationship to Velocity Vibes

Velocity Vibes defines how the estate builds: founder intent, architecture control, scoped agent execution, deployment validation, and continuous refinement.

Imperium defines what deserves to be built or invested in.

Velocity Vibes is the delivery method.

Imperium is the venture judgement canon.

## Relationship to Roman naming doctrine

Imperium does not replace Ordo Animi, Valour, Custos, or Magister Automatorum.

It sits above product naming as a decision canon.

The Roman/Latin language layer is not decoration. It creates structure, memory, and continuity across the estate.

## Operating rule

No future product line should move from concept into productisation without an Imperium review.

If the product cannot explain its Incendium, Machina, Pecunia, Ecosystema, and Regio, it is not ready to become a standalone app or venture.
